# 14. Rules for CV Generation

This section is for the CV-generation agent rather than the professional profile itself.

1. Use only information contained in this document.
2. Do not invent technologies, responsibilities, metrics, titles, certifications, or achievements.
3. Do not include every technology in every CV.
4. Select experience based on the target job description.
5. Prioritize measurable achievements over generic responsibilities.
6. Avoid repeating the same achievement in multiple sections.
7. Do not describe a technology as "expert" unless the source explicitly supports that level of claim.
8. Do not include empty sections.
9. Do not mention information listed under "Missing / Optional Information" unless it has been provided later.
10. Adapt the professional summary to the target role rather than storing fixed summaries here.
11. Keep OpenRouter organization management associated with AHBS employment and represent it as a separate project or initiative only when relevant to the target role.
12. Avoid duplicating the same OpenRouter responsibilities across employment, projects, skills, and achievements sections.

## ATS and Job-Description Alignment Rules

1. Carefully analyze the complete job application before writing the CV, including the job title, responsibilities, required qualifications, preferred qualifications, technical requirements, domain terminology, seniority expectations, location requirements, and any application-specific instructions.
2. Identify the exact keywords and phrases used in the job description, including technologies, programming languages, frameworks, methodologies, certifications, soft skills, industry terms, and role-specific responsibilities.
3. Map each important job-description requirement to verified evidence in this profile before including it in the CV.
4. Use the employer’s terminology where it accurately matches documented experience. Include common equivalent terms only when they are truthful and useful for ATS matching.
5. Prioritize keywords that appear repeatedly, in required qualifications, in core responsibilities, or in the job title.
6. Include relevant keywords naturally in the professional summary, skills section, employment bullets, and project descriptions where supported by evidence.
7. Do not add keywords solely to improve ATS matching if the profile does not provide evidence of actual experience with them.
8. Do not keyword-stuff, repeat the same keyword unnaturally, or create a separate keyword list that is not supported by the CV content.
9. Use standard ATS-readable section headings such as Professional Summary, Technical Skills, Professional Experience, Projects, Education, and Publications when generating the final CV.
10. Use simple, machine-readable formatting: standard text, consistent dates, clear job titles, conventional bullet points, and readable section order.
11. Avoid tables, text boxes, columns, graphics, icons, images, headers, footers, decorative symbols, and unusual formatting in the final CV unless the user explicitly requests an ATS-compatible format that supports them.
12. Spell out important acronyms at least once when appropriate, followed by the acronym in parentheses, such as Model Context Protocol (MCP), OpenID Connect (OIDC), or Application Programming Interface (API).
13. Preserve exact technology names and versions when they are relevant to the target role, such as .NET, ASP.NET Core, .NET Framework, Consul, Temporal, RabbitMQ, Neo4j, LangGraph, CrewAI, OpenAI, and OpenRouter.
14. Match the CV’s seniority, scope, and wording to the job application. Do not present documented participation as ownership, or technical contribution as people management.
15. Select the most relevant experience and achievements rather than attempting to include the entire profile.
16. Ensure every major required qualification is either supported by a clearly visible CV entry or is omitted because it cannot be verified from this profile.
17. After drafting the CV, perform an ATS-focused review against the job application:

    * Confirm that the target job title or a truthful equivalent is clearly represented.
    * Confirm that the most important required technologies and competencies appear where supported.
    * Confirm that relevant responsibilities are demonstrated through experience bullets.
    * Confirm that measurable achievements are included when relevant.
    * Confirm that no unsupported requirements or technologies were added.
    * Confirm that terminology is consistent throughout the CV.
    * Confirm that dates, employers, locations, and employment periods are accurate.
    * Confirm that the document is readable by standard ATS parsers.
18. Compare the final CV against the job application requirement by requirement and identify any important requirement that is missing, weakly evidenced, or only indirectly represented.
19. If the user requests an ATS score, provide an evidence-based assessment based on keyword coverage, requirement alignment, experience relevance, formatting compatibility, and factual accuracy. Do not claim a guaranteed score or guaranteed interview outcome.
20. If the job application is incomplete, unclear, or unavailable, ask for the full job description before performing detailed ATS optimization. Do not infer missing requirements from the job title alone.

# 14c. Selling-Point Rule

Before writing the CV, headline, or cover letter for an application, build a **selling-point map** and record it under `## NOT PART OF LETTER — notes` in `Cover_Letter.md`. The map drives the CV summary, the bullet selection, the headline, and the cover-letter hook.

## Method

1. **Read the JD for demand signals** — not just the requirements list. Look at: the job title, the "why we're hiring / about the team" paragraph, pain points ("mature codebase", "recent security audit", "scaling"), repeated themes, and the *nice-to-haves* (they reveal what the team actually values).
2. **Match each signal to Moamen's strongest asset in that area** from the inventory below. Only use assets backed by evidence in this profile.
3. **Rank.** Pick the **1–2 highest-leverage selling points** — where Moamen is genuinely strong *and* the company clearly cares. These lead the headline and the cover-letter first paragraph, and shape the CV professional summary. Support with 2–4 more in the body. Do not try to sell every asset; a letter that sells everything sells nothing.
4. **State the differentiator plainly.** Moamen's edge is an unusual *combination* for ~3 years of experience: enterprise backend + distributed-systems architecture + production AI/agentic engineering + large-scale legacy modernization + a regulated domain (healthcare). Most candidates have one of these. Identify which intersection this company needs and make that the story.

# 15. Achievement Selection Priority

When generating a CV, prefer achievements in this order:

1. Quantified business/technical impact
2. Architecture ownership
3. Complex engineering problems
4. Production systems
5. Large-scale modernization
6. Infrastructure/platform work
7. Cross-team impact
8. General implementation work

Avoid filling the CV with low-value statements such as:

* "Worked with..."
* "Responsible for..."
* "Used..."
* "Participated in..."
* "Developed features..."

unless they provide useful context for a more significant achievement.
